Beehiiv shows up in a different kind of conversation than Kit, Mailchimp, or MailerLite. Ask a blogger which email tool to use and you'll hear Kit or MailerLite. Ask a newsletter writer the same question and Beehiiv comes up constantly, usually alongside a screenshot of someone's subscriber-revenue dashboard. That's not an accident: Beehiiv was built by former Morning Brew people specifically to help newsletters make money, and it shows in what the product prioritizes over Kit.

This comparison is for anyone deciding between the two, whether you're a blogger whose email list is starting to feel like the actual business, or a newsletter writer weighing whether Kit's bigger free tier is worth giving up Beehiiv's built-in monetization tools.

## Pricing and free plans

Kit's free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers, with unlimited landing pages, forms, and broadcast emails, though automation stays locked behind a paid plan regardless of list size. Beehiiv's free Launch plan caps out much lower, at 2,500 subscribers, but bundles in a full newsletter website and podcast hosting alongside email, tools Kit doesn't offer.

Once you're ready to pay, the two plans solve different problems. Kit's Creator plan starts around $33/month at 1,000 subscribers and unlocks unlimited automations and sequences immediately, with pricing that climbs as your subscriber count grows past that. Beehiiv's Scale plan runs about $43/month and covers up to 100,000 subscribers on that single price, along with automation, an ad network, paid subscriptions, and digital product sales.

That gap matters more than it looks at first glance. A blogger with a 5,000-person list pays Kit roughly $33-66/month depending on where they land in Kit's per-1,000-subscriber tiers, while the same list on Beehiiv's Scale plan is still $43/month flat, with room to grow to 100,000 subscribers before the price changes at all. If your list is small and you want the longest possible free runway, Kit's 10,000-subscriber free tier wins outright. If you expect real growth and want your paid plan to absorb that growth without repricing every few thousand subscribers, Beehiiv's flat ceiling is the more predictable bet.

## What each tool is actually for

Kit is an email tool with a content-creator lens: tags instead of separate lists, forms and landing pages, and a workflow built around growing and monetizing a list through email. It doesn't try to be your website or your podcast host.

Beehiiv is built around the newsletter as the product, not just the list behind it. Every plan includes a hosted website for your publication, and Beehiiv doubles as podcast hosting too, so a newsletter writer can run their site, their email list, and their podcast feed from one account instead of stitching together WordPress, an email tool, and a separate podcast host. If your blog and your newsletter are effectively the same thing, that consolidation is a real reason to pick Beehiiv over Kit, which assumes you already have a website living somewhere else.

The tradeoff is focus. Kit's narrower scope means less to learn and fewer moving parts if all you want is email. Beehiiv's website and podcast tools are useful if you need them, but they're extra surface area to configure if you don't, and Kit's simpler mental model (write email, grow list, tag people) is easier to pick up on day one.

## Automation

Kit's automations are unlimited on any paid plan from the start, with a visual builder for welcome sequences, tag-based triggers, and RSS campaigns, and no step-count ceiling to hit as your funnel gets more complex.

Beehiiv gates automation entirely behind its Scale plan and above; the free Launch plan has none. Once you're on Scale, Beehiiv's automation covers welcome sequences and tag-based flows similar to Kit's, with more advanced click-triggered automation reserved for the higher Max plan. For a newsletter that's still on the free tier, this means no automated welcome sequence at all until you upgrade, the same gap Kit has on its own free plan.

Neither tool gives you meaningful automation for free. The real difference is what you get once you do pay: Kit's automation is included from its first paid tier, while Beehiiv holds some of its more advanced automation back for an even higher plan than Scale.

## Monetization: Beehiiv's real edge

If there's one category where Beehiiv beats Kit outright for the right kind of creator, it's monetization built into the platform itself.

Beehiiv's ad network connects newsletter publishers with sponsors directly inside the platform, so a creator can earn sponsorship revenue without cold-emailing brands or working through a separate marketplace. Its paid subscriptions run on what Beehiiv calls a 0% take rate, meaning Beehiiv doesn't skim a cut of subscription revenue beyond Stripe's standard processing fee. Digital product sales work the same way.

Kit supports paid newsletters, digital products, and recurring subscriptions too, but at a 3.5% plus $0.30 transaction fee on top of whatever your payment processor charges. Kit has also added Paid Recommendations and newsletter sponsorships on its Creator and Pro plans, but there's no built-in ad marketplace matching Beehiiv's; connecting with sponsors on Kit is still largely a do-it-yourself process.

For a creator earning real money from a list, that fee difference and the built-in ad network add up to an ongoing cost difference, not just a feature checkbox. If monetization is the whole point of your list, this is the section of the comparison to weigh most heavily.

## So which one should you actually pick?

Kit is the better fit if you're a blogger who wants email to support a site you already have, values the biggest possible free tier while your list is still small, and doesn't need built-in monetization tools beyond a simple digital product or two. Its narrower focus means less to configure and a shorter learning curve.

Beehiiv is the better fit if your newsletter is the product, not a supplement to a blog elsewhere, and especially if monetization is the goal rather than an afterthought. A flat-rate plan that scales to 100,000 subscribers, a built-in ad network, and zero-take-rate subscriptions add up to a different deal than Kit offers, even though Beehiiv's free tier is smaller and automation costs more to unlock.

Neither is a wrong choice. It comes down to whether you're growing a list to support content you already publish elsewhere, or building the newsletter itself into a standalone, monetized publication.
