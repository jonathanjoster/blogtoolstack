Past that, they solve different problems, and the right one depends on how big your list is, how much automation you actually need, and whether you'd rather pay by subscriber count or a flat fee.

## Kit

Kit (formerly ConvertKit) was built for people who make a living from an audience: writers, podcasters, course creators, bloggers. That focus shows up in the free plan, which covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, forms, and broadcast emails. Very few tools on this list let a list grow that large before asking for a credit card.

The tradeoff is that automations and paid newsletters sit behind the paid Creator plan, which starts around $33 a month at 1,000 subscribers. Once you're there, automations are unlimited with no step caps to bump into later, and Kit throws in free migration help if you're bringing a list over from somewhere else. The interface is also narrower than some competitors here: there's no built-in website builder, and Kit's Commerce feature covers simple digital-product and subscription sales but not a full storefront, so running an actual online store still means pairing Kit with another tool.

Kit makes the most sense for a blogger or newsletter writer who is mainly trying to grow and eventually monetize a list through content, and who doesn't mind keeping a separate tool for anything outside of email.

## Mailchimp

Mailchimp is the name most new bloggers already know, mostly because it's been around since before "email marketing tool" was a common phrase. It's a genuinely capable platform, especially once you start selling products online, since its ecommerce integrations and ad tools go further than anything else on this list.

The free plan caps out at 250 contacts and 500 sends a month with no automation included, and the paid Essentials plan starting around $13 a month looks cheap until you read the fine print on how billing actually works. Mailchimp counts unsubscribed contacts toward your billed total unless you go in and archive them yourself, so a list that churns can quietly cost you for people who no longer open a single email from you. Because pricing scales with contact count rather than usage, a growing blog can watch its bill climb steadily even though nothing about how the list is used has changed.

This one tends to fit best for someone who already runs a small online store and wants email tied into the same dashboard as their ads and product catalog, or someone with a genuinely small list who plans to stay small for a while.

## MailerLite

MailerLite gets recommended constantly as "the cheaper Kit," which is close to true on price but misses what actually makes it different. Its free plan covers 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month with 3 automations, one website, and one landing page. Past that, the Comfort plan runs about $12 a month for 50 automations and up to 10 websites and landing pages, and the Power plan at around $25 a month removes the caps entirely.

The real reason to consider MailerLite is not the price, though. It includes an actual website and landing-page builder as a standard part of the product rather than an add-on, with a drag-and-drop grid editor, RSS-to-email campaigns, and native WordPress integration. Anyone without a site yet, or who wants a fast landing page for a lead magnet without spinning up separate hosting, can cover that from the same account they already use for email. The builder is still grid-based rather than a full CMS, so if you want granular control over every element's placement, you'll eventually hit its limits.

It's a strong pick for a new blogger who doesn't have a website yet, or for anyone who'd rather run email and a simple site from one login instead of two separate tools.

## ActiveCampaign

ActiveCampaign is the outlier on this list because it wasn't built primarily for bloggers. It's a marketing automation and light CRM platform first, with email as one piece of a larger system, and its automation builder supports branching logic, lead scoring, and sales-pipeline features that go well past what a typical newsletter needs.

That depth is real, and it's genuinely useful if you're running affiliate funnels or evaluating leads before a sales conversation. It's also the most expensive tool on this list at comparable list sizes and generally positioned as a premium option, and there's a real learning curve: the flexibility that makes the automation builder powerful also means more decisions just to set up something as simple as a welcome sequence. There's no permanent free plan, only a trial.

If you've outgrown simple welcome sequences and want complex, multi-branch automation or CRM-style features, and you're willing to pay more and spend more time learning the tool, ActiveCampaign is worth a look.

## Flodesk

Flodesk takes a different approach to pricing than everything else on this list: one flat monthly fee, regardless of how many subscribers you have. If your list is growing quickly, that's a real advantage over contact-based pricing, since your bill doesn't creep up every time you add a hundred new subscribers.

Flodesk is also built around design first. Its email and landing-page templates are some of the more polished, visually driven options around, which matters if your blog leans on strong visuals or a distinct brand look. Workflows, its version of automations, are included even on the entry plan rather than gated behind a higher tier, which is a real point in its favor over Kit or Mailchimp. The catch is that it's lighter on advanced segmentation, reporting, and third-party integrations than the other tools here, and there's no permanent free plan, just a trial period.

A design-conscious blogger who wants attractive emails without fighting a clunky template builder, and who'd rather pay a flat fee than the deepest automation or analytics, will probably get the most out of Flodesk.

## How to actually choose

Start with two questions. How big is your list right now, and what do you actually need beyond sending a newsletter?

If your list is small and you don't have a website yet, MailerLite's combined email-and-site builder removes a whole separate purchase from your plate. If you're squarely focused on growing and monetizing an audience through content and don't mind keeping email as its own tool, Kit's free tier goes further before you pay anything, and its automation and sequence features are built around exactly that goal. If you already run a store, Mailchimp's ecommerce ties may outweigh its billing quirks. If you're past simple welcome sequences and need branching logic or lead scoring, ActiveCampaign is worth the steeper price and the learning curve. And if design is what sells your brand, Flodesk's flat pricing and template quality are hard to beat.

None of these are wrong choices on their own terms. They're built for different list sizes and different jobs, and the tool that fits a blogger with 300 subscribers and no site isn't the same one that fits a blogger with 8,000 subscribers running affiliate funnels. Match the tool to where your list actually is today, not where you hope it'll be in two years. You can always switch, or add a second tool, once your needs change.
