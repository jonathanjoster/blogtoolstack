If Kit and Mailchimp are the two names every new blogger stumbles into first, MailerLite is the one that shows up next, usually from someone in a Facebook group insisting it's "basically the same as Kit but cheaper." That's not quite true, and it's not quite false either. The two tools solve overlapping problems in different ways, and which one wins depends more on what else you need besides email than on price alone.

This comparison sticks to the blogger and niche-site-owner use case: growing a list, sending newsletters, running a welcome sequence, maybe selling a small digital product on the side. We'll go through pricing, ease of use, automation, and support, then land on a verdict that doesn't just crown Kit again, because MailerLite outright beats it on at least one front that matters for a lot of bloggers.

## Pricing and free plans

MailerLite's free plan covers up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, with 3 automations, one website, one landing page, and community-only support. Kit's free plan is far more generous on raw subscriber count: up to 10,000 subscribers, with unlimited landing pages, forms, and broadcast emails, though automations stay locked behind a paid plan either way.

Once you outgrow free, MailerLite's Comfort plan starts around $12/month and adds email support, 50 active automations, and up to 10 websites and landing pages. Its Power plan runs about $25/month and removes the caps entirely: unlimited automations, unlimited websites and landing pages, unlimited user seats, and 24/7 live chat. Kit's Creator plan starts around $33/month at 1,000 subscribers and includes unlimited automations and sequences right away, no step-count ceiling to bump into later.

So at small list sizes, MailerLite is cheaper in dollar terms. But the comparison isn't purely about the monthly invoice. Kit's 10,000-subscriber free ceiling means a blogger can grow an actual, sizable list, everything except automation, without paying anything, while MailerLite's free plan caps out at 250 subscribers before you're pushed toward a paid tier just to keep collecting new signups. If your priority is the longest possible free runway while your list is still small, Kit's free tier goes further. If your priority is the cheapest possible paid plan once you're ready to pay for automation, MailerLite's Comfort tier undercuts Kit's Creator plan by a wide margin.

## Ease of use, and MailerLite's real advantage

Both tools are built to be approachable for non-technical users, and both largely succeed at the basics: signup forms, list management, and a drag-and-drop email editor that doesn't require any code.

Where they diverge is scope. Kit is narrowly focused on email and the things that support it, forms, landing pages, and a simple link-tagging system for segmentation. MailerLite goes further and includes an actual website and landing-page builder as a standard part of the product, not a bolted-on extra. You can build a full site, a blog, or a single landing page with a drag-and-drop grid editor, apply pre-made templates, and connect Stripe to sell a digital product directly, all inside the same account you use for email.

I'll say this plainly, because it's the strongest case for MailerLite over Kit for a certain kind of blogger. If you don't have a WordPress site yet, or you want a fast, no-separate-hosting landing page for a lead magnet or a one-off promotion, MailerLite can cover that need without a second tool or a second monthly bill. Kit has landing pages too, but MailerLite's builder is more capable as a general-purpose site tool, largely because that's what a meaningful chunk of its product roadmap is built around, including RSS-to-email campaigns and native WordPress integration aimed squarely at content creators.

The tradeoff is that MailerLite's builder is still a grid-based system, not a full CMS. Several reviewers who've used it at length describe the layout customization as more constrained than a "real" website builder or WordPress theme, fine for a landing page or a simple blog, less fine if you want granular control over every element's placement. Put plainly: MailerLite's website builder is a real value-add if you need one tool to do double duty, not a replacement for WordPress if you already have a real site with real design requirements.

## Automation and sequence features

Kit's automations are unlimited on any paid plan from day one: no step caps, no tier where you have to upgrade again just to remove a ceiling. Building a welcome sequence, delivering a lead magnet, or tagging someone based on what they click is a smooth, well-documented process, and it's clearly the part of the product Kit has invested the most in, since sequences are core to how creators actually make money from a list.

MailerLite's automation builder is capable for straightforward use cases, welcome sequences, basic drip campaigns, tag-based triggers, and its templates make the common scenarios quick to set up. But it's less deep once you look past the basics. It lacks more advanced triggers like scoring a lead based on behavior or, until higher tiers, tracking specific page visits, and the automation count itself is capped below the unlimited Power plan (3 free, 50 on Comfort). For a blogger whose automation needs stop at "welcome sequence plus a couple of tagged follow-ups," that ceiling rarely gets hit. For anyone planning more elaborate, multi-branch flows, Kit's uncapped approach ages better as your strategy gets more complex.

## Customer support

Kit offers 24/7 email and chat support on paid plans, plus free migration help when you're bringing an existing list over from another provider, which removes a real chunk of the friction and tedium of switching tools.

MailerLite's support is tiered more sharply by plan. Free users get community support only, no live agent. Comfort adds email support, and 24/7 live chat doesn't kick in until the Power plan. There's also no dedicated migration assistance from MailerLite itself, so moving an existing list and rebuilding automations is more of a do-it-yourself job, or one you outsource. A few reviewers and users have also flagged MailerLite's account approval and anti-spam enforcement as strict enough to occasionally lock accounts with little warning, which is worth knowing about before you build your whole list-growth plan around a platform, though this isn't something every user runs into.

Kit's support model is more consistent across its paid tiers, and its migration assistance is a real, practical edge if you're not starting from zero.

## So which one should you actually pick?

Kit is the better fit if your priority is growing and monetizing a list through content: a big free tier while you're small, unlimited automations the moment you go paid, and support that includes migration help if you're coming from somewhere else. It's built specifically around what bloggers and newsletter writers do.

MailerLite is the better fit if you value a cheaper paid entry point and, more importantly, if you want an all-in-one tool that also builds your landing pages or even a simple website without adding WordPress or a separate site builder to your stack. Its free-to-use website and landing-page builder, RSS campaign automation, and WordPress integration make it a strong option for a blogger who wants fewer tools in their toolkit, not just a cheaper email plan.

If you're brand new and don't have a site yet, or you want one dashboard to handle email, a landing page, and a simple digital-product sale, MailerLite's breadth is a real reason to choose it over Kit. If your site already exists and email growth and automation depth are what you actually need more of, Kit's bigger free tier and uncapped automations make it the more durable choice as your list and your strategy grow.

Neither answer is wrong. It comes down to whether you're solving an email problem or a "I have too many separate tools" problem.
